# Phrase blends and vocal/backing mashups

In DJ → Transition options, choose an 8-, 16-, or 32-beat phrase launch grid. The deck section controls expose the same saved choice. Incoming entry phase is matched to the outgoing phrase phase on the audio clock, with tempo matched before launch. A manually selected entry is retained. Your seconds-based mix time is unchanged. For an automatically selected energy section with sufficient preceding audio, the engine introduces the incoming song one fade-length before the selected section. With a matching phrase grid, the selected section lands at the corresponding outgoing phrase boundary as the fade completes. Explicit/manual cues retain their selected entry; unavailable pre-roll uses the existing entry.

Library → Prepare → Mark phrase start at cursor saves a reviewed phrase anchor. Save preparation and reload the deck to use it. Reviewed phrase anchors take priority, then reviewed bar anchors, then the existing beat phase as an estimated counted grid. DJ labels unreviewed phrasing as estimated. Variable-tempo songs, automatic verse/chorus detection and vocal-line recognition are not implemented. Phrase alignment of different tracks requires their effective tempos to match; unavailable grids or insufficient remaining time use a normal blend.

Enable Automatic vocal / instrumental mashups to include the technique in Auto DJ. Select its style to use it at the saved effect frequency, or use Mashup next pair for one handoff. Choose an 8-, 16-, or 32-beat hold. The hold is separate from the saved entry/exit fade seconds, so the total transition is two fades plus the hold.

The entry fade brings in the incoming track's instrumental/drum stems while removing outgoing backing. During the hold, outgoing vocals play over incoming backing; incoming vocals remain masked. The exit fade hands over vocals and stops the outgoing deck. There are still two main decks. Temporary masks multiply existing live stem gains; muted/isolation defaults are never automatically unmuted or written to metadata. Knobs and their saved values remain unchanged, and cancellation removes temporary masks.

Mashups require both prepared stem sets, phase-capable constant-tempo grids, a paused incoming deck, sufficient remaining audio, audible selected stems, and compatible effective tempo/tuning. Automatic pairing conservatively accepts the same Camelot number (including relative major/minor) and rejects significant varispeed detuning. When the installed Key Lock backend is active on a deck, compatibility uses its preserved tuning instead of treating its tempo change as a pitch change. If Key Lock is unavailable, the conservative varispeed check remains. The next-song choice prefers compatible prepared songs among the top twelve energy-ranked eligible candidates. Playlist membership, repeats, requested songs and existing source restrictions retain priority. An unsuitable pair falls back to a normal blend with a status explanation.

Separation quality and vocal phrasing still need listening judgment. The engine does not determine whether a specific lyric line suits the other backing or guarantee audible vocals throughout an instrumental passage.

Validation: workspace tests and strict Clippy; phrase-origin/phase tests; numeric stem-routing checks against known component amplitudes; cancellation and incoming-failure recovery; persistence/opt-in checks; and a muted native FFmpeg test launching both prepared stem decoders on a phrase grid through the complete handoff.

## Pair-planning refinement — November 12, 2026

Desktop Auto DJ and headless Smart Radio share bounded half/double-time tempo matching. A 70/140 BPM pair can retain approximately normal playback speed rather than attempting a 2x speed change. Desktop retains its 16% limit and headless radio its 8% limit. Ranking considers half/double-time distance without widening playlist eligibility. Ambiguous half-time pairs may align beats; they do not claim matching bar/phrase structure. Phrase launch grids now require compatible effective playback tempos.

Pre-roll is a loaded-session change, never a shared metadata write. It respects approved entry bounds, manual cues, unstable-grid rejection and the exact saved fade duration. Short sources without room for the introduction keep their existing entry. Headless radio can shorten overlap when a source is too short, as before. An unreadable prefetched candidate may fall back to another song with an ordinary blend.

Verification includes a rendered tone/marked-drop handoff at 1 kHz, the native muted FFmpeg pre-roll handoff with unchanged saved preparation and a retained three-second fade, the existing native early-handoff test, and the encoded Smart Radio bad-file/repeated-handoff test. These establish timing and continuity under isolated fixtures; they are not a listening evaluation of arbitrary real music or a long-duration broadcast soak. Semantic downbeat/verse/chorus detection, vocal-line compatibility and variable-tempo tracking remain future work.
